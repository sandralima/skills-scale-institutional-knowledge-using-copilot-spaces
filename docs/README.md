# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to the OctoAcme Project Management guide. This documentation centralizes the processes, roles, and best practices used to deliver projects successfully.

## Quick Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured, iterative project management approach centered on customer value, clear ownership, and data-informed decisions. Our framework spans the entire project lifecycle—from initial concept through retrospective and continuous improvement—with defined roles, communication cadences, and decision gates.

### Core Principles
- **Customer-first**: Prioritize customer value and usability
- **Iterative delivery**: Deliver small, testable increments
- **Clear ownership**: Each project has a named Project Manager and Product Lead
- **Data-informed decisions**: Measure impact and iterate based on evidence
- **Psychological safety**: Encourage feedback and learning

## OctoAcme Project Management Process Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured lifecycle that moves from initiation to planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement. New work begins with a project one-pager that clarifies the problem, goals, success metrics, stakeholders, and initial risks, and a decision gate determines when the effort is ready to move into planning. Planning then turns the idea into a backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, milestones, and dependencies, with a clear definition of done to keep delivery on track. Execution focuses on day-to-day momentum through standups, weekly syncs, sprint reviews, and a board workflow that moves work from backlog to done.

The framework clearly defines key personas and responsibilities to keep work coordinated and accountable. Product or project leads set outcomes and prioritize the backlog, while PMs manage schedules, risks, documentation, and communications across the team and stakeholders. Developers build and test features, QA validates acceptance and quality, and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. This division of responsibility helps keep ownership clear and makes it easier to identify who owns decisions, risks, and dependencies as the project progresses.

Communication is treated as a continuous discipline rather than a one-time activity. OctoAcme recommends weekly PM + product alignment, twice-weekly or daily team standups, demos at milestone boundaries, and milestone-based stakeholder updates. Risk and blocker communication is formalized through escalation paths, from team-level triage to PM and product leadership, and for urgent or business-critical issues, sponsor-level escalation is used. A single source of truth—such as the project README or release doc—supports consistency, while weekly status templates and incident communication templates help teams share progress, decisions, and actions clearly.

Quality assurance is built into each stage of the process. The team is expected to use pull requests with defined requirements, require approvals and CI checks, and ensure acceptance criteria are captured before work is merged. Quality practices include unit and integration tests, smoke tests for critical flows, security scanning, and manual QA when needed. Releases follow a defined checklist with pre-release requirements, deployment verification, rollback planning, and post-deploy validation, while retrospectives turn lessons learned into action items tracked through the backlog or issue system. This creates a repeatable process that balances speed, transparency, and reliability as work moves from idea to production.

## Key Communication Cadence

- **Weekly sync** between PM + Product Manager
- **Twice-weekly standups** for delivery team (or as agreed)
- **Monthly stakeholder updates**
- **Ad-hoc escalations** as needed
